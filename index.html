What can this device sense?

Every phone, tablet and laptop has different sensors, and every browser lets websites use a different subset of them. This page checks yours live. Tap a card to open that scanner.

Permissions

The app always asks you first, explains what a sensor is used for, and only then lets the browser show its own permission prompt. You can change your mind here at any time.

What each kind of sensor can do in this app. Live = used directly when your browser allows it. Add‑on = works by connecting an external Bluetooth / USB sensor or another app. Blocked = the hardware may exist, but no browser lets websites read it.

Best browsers

  • Android: Chrome or Samsung Internet unlock the most (Bluetooth, flashlight, magnetometer, sonar).
  • iPhone / iPad: Safari supports camera, sonar, ultrasonic, compass and motion. Apple blocks Web Bluetooth in every iOS browser; use the free Bluefy browser app to get Bluetooth.
  • Windows / Mac / Linux: Chrome or Edge for Bluetooth. Firefox and Safari work for camera, sonar and ultrasonic.

Honest limits

Browsers cannot read raw Wi‑Fi scans, cell radio, UWB, Soli radar, the barometer or the iPhone LiDAR directly, and most phones have no thermal camera. Where something is blocked we say so instead of faking it, and offer the closest real alternative (AR depth, AI depth, external sensors, or importing from another app). No scanner can guarantee a room is free of cameras: use this as one tool alongside a careful physical check. Only scan places you are entitled to inspect.

3D scanner: rooms & objects

Scan a whole room, or a single object like a shoe, mug, plant or sculpture, then download it as a 3D model for Blender, 3D printing, AR or games. Drag the 3D view to orbit, pinch or scroll to zoom.

Object scanning

  • Best: object on a plain table, good even light, nothing shiny or see‑through. A lazy Susan or a piece of paper you rotate works as a turntable.
  • AR depth (Android): point at the object first so it locks on, then walk slowly all the way around it.
  • AI depth (any device): keep the phone still, take a photo, turn the object by the same angle, repeat until it's gone all the way round (12 photos at 30°).

Why do I only see one device?

Full room scans use Chrome's Bluetooth Scanning feature, which is still behind a switch. On Android or desktop Chrome/Edge open chrome://flags/#enable-experimental-web-platform-features, set it to Enabled and restart the browser. Without it, browsers only let you pick one device at a time from a list (which still shows every nearby device's name).

Hidden camera finder

Every camera has a lens that reflects light straight back at its source, like a cat's eye. This mode turns on your flashlight, watches the live image and circles tiny bright reflections that stay put while you move. Night‑vision cameras also glow with infrared LEDs that your camera can see but your eyes can't.

How to sweep a room

  1. Turn the room lights off and close the curtains.
  2. Hold the phone at eye level and turn slowly, pointing at smoke detectors, clocks, chargers, vents, plants, shelves, TVs, mirrors and anything facing the bed or shower.
  3. A real lens shows as a small, sharp point that stays bright as you move a little. Glass and metal glints usually vanish when you shift.
  4. IR mode: in the dark, many front cameras (selfie side) show IR LEDs as purple‑white dots. Point the screen side around the room.

Magnetic field (EMF) detector

Uses the phone's compass chip. Powered electronics, motors, speakers, transformers and many hidden cameras make a local magnetic field. Calibrate away from electronics, then move the top of the phone slowly over walls, objects and furniture. A sudden jump marks something with a magnet or current inside.

Sonar room mapper

Your speaker plays a short chirp and your microphone listens for the echo, like a bat. The delay gives the distance to the nearest wall or large object in the direction the phone is facing. Stand in the middle of the room, point the phone (speaker side) at a wall, ping, turn, ping again. The dots trace the room's shape.

On phones with a compass the heading updates by itself. On laptops, drag the heading slider to the direction you're pointing. Accuracy is roughly ±20 cm in quiet rooms; soft furniture and curtains swallow echoes.

Ultrasonic & electronic noise

A live spectrogram from the microphone up to the highest pitch it can hear (usually 20–24 kHz). Switching power supplies, some cameras, tracking beacons and ultrasonic "audio beacons" leave thin bright lines above 15 kHz, above most adults' hearing.

Presence detector (ultrasonic Doppler)

The speaker plays a steady 19 kHz tone most adults can't hear. Anything moving in the room — a person, a door, a pet — shifts the echo's pitch slightly (Doppler effect), which shows up as motion. Put the phone down, start it, and stay still. Pets may hear it, so keep sessions short.

Light: brightness, colour temperature & flicker

Uses the light sensor when the browser exposes it, otherwise the camera. Shows how bright the room is, whether the lighting is warm or cool (in kelvin), and whether lamps or screens are flickering. Hidden devices with status LEDs can show up as a sudden flicker in a dark room.

Thermal view

No phone or laptop has a heat sensor that websites can read. Plug in a USB thermal camera that works as a webcam (UVC; many FLIR, InfiRay, TOPDON and HIKMICRO models do) and pick it below to see real heat signatures in false colour. With an ordinary camera this view only colours brightness, not temperature.

Network signal

Browsers aren't allowed to list Wi‑Fi networks or read raw radio strength. What they can do: report your connection type and speed, and measure round‑trip time. Walk around while this runs: latency and speed get worse where the Wi‑Fi or mobile signal is weak, so you can find dead spots and the direction of the router.

Want real Wi‑Fi and device scanning?

Scanning every Wi‑Fi network and every device on the network needs an installed app, not a website. On Android, apps like WiFiAnalyzer (Wi‑Fi strength) and Fing (devices on your network, often including cameras) do this. On a laptop, your router's admin page lists every connected device.

Motion, level, vibration & GPS

Accelerometer and gyroscope show which way is down, how level a surface is and tiny vibrations (footsteps, machines, appliances behind a wall). GPS gives position, altitude and speed.

NFC tags & add‑on sensors

Read NFC tags hidden in objects, and connect external sensors over Bluetooth or USB to add what phones can't do on the web: air quality, humidity, pressure, temperature, mmWave radar presence, thermal arrays, Geiger counters and more.

NFC tag readerchecking…

Hold the back of the phone against stickers, posters, cards and objects. Finds hidden NFC tags and shows what they contain. Android Chrome / Samsung Internet.

Bluetooth environment sensorchecking…

Connects to any Bluetooth sensor that uses the standard Environmental Sensing profile: temperature, humidity, pressure, UV, gas, wind and more. Many cheap thermo‑hygrometers and DIY ESP32 boards work.

USB / serial sensorchecking…

Plug in an Arduino, ESP32 or sensor board that prints readings like co2=612 voc=31 or JSON lines. Works with mmWave radar (e.g. LD2410), MLX90640 thermal arrays, PM2.5 / VOC / CO₂ sensors, Geiger counters.

Example sketch for an ESP32 / Arduino sensor 
void setup() { Serial.begin(115200); }
void loop() {
  float t = readTemp(), h = readHumidity();
  Serial.print("temp_c="); Serial.print(t);
  Serial.print(" humidity="); Serial.println(h);
  delay(500);
}

Any name=value pairs or a JSON object per line will appear as live tiles above and are saved in your scan export.
